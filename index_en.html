Abstract

The present interactive map of the soil biota of Argentina compiles all available information on Clitellata (earthworms), Acari (mites), and Collembola (springtails) reported in scientific publications and communications. Each point represents a georeferenced mesofauna sampling site when such information was available, or its most precise possible location according to the descriptions provided by each author. Each color corresponds to one of the three sampled edaphic fauna groups. By hovering over any of the points, a dialog box displays all relevant information, including the complete citation and the authors associated with each record. The information was collected through bibliographic searches, consultations in libraries, and direct contact with authors and researchers. A previous, static version of this map was published in the Global Biodiversity Information Facility (GBIF) and is available at: https://www.gbif.org/dataset/b863efea-ab18-47a6-bf8e-65fa8962a18a.

For the entire territory of Argentina, only 227 scientific publications containing data on these mesofauna groups were found. The map clearly highlights the scarcity of available data given the country’s vast geographical extent. It also shows how research efforts tend to form clusters around locations where researchers are based and, in several cases, how sampling was conducted along roads used as transects. Large areas of the territory remain completely devoid of information

This is the first work to compile, within a single publication, all georeferenced occurrence data for earthworms (oligochaeta), mites, and springtails recorded to date in Argentina, with full indication of all sources, authors, and citations. A complete description of the methods and the full dataset, can be found in Sanabria et al. 2023.

Study area

The sites where the relevant taxa were found are in the Neotropical Region, on the continent of South America, specifically in the Argentine Republic. Its extension is 3,669,711 km², including terrestrial areas and maritime zones such as the South Orkney Islands and the South Atlantic islands. The country has a wide surface coverage; therefore, it also has important climatic diversity, ranging from the tropical climates of the Chaco, Tucumán-Oranense and Misiones ecoregions, to the cold and dry climate of Patagonia.
